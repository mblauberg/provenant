# Provenant v1.6.24

Released 9 November 2026.

Reviewers now share one definition of a blocking finding, and chairs weigh the structural fix beside the small one and treat docs as evidence of a past decision. Finished lanes clean up after themselves, cloud jobs can write to a named branch, and lane supervision costs less.

## Behaviour changes

### Accounts, quota and routing

- Crucial review floors now cover only credential and secret paths, release, publish, deploy and auto-merge workflows, `CODEOWNERS`, and this repository's own boundary modules, each with its reason in `review-defaults.toml`. Paths that matched only a word such as `build`, `object` or `chair` fall back to their other classes, and a large change no longer selects the `deep` review shape by size alone; integration heads and boundary paths still do (#3081).
- The bundled `senior` pool offers `codex/gpt-6.1-terra` only at low or medium effort, with the weight moved to `codex/gpt-6.1-sol`, because terra at high effort stopped writers early more often than the other routes. An exact `codex/gpt-6.1-terra@high` route and pool overrides in configuration still work (#3074).

### Pipelines and graphs

- Reviewers apply one definition of blocking, delivered to every lens: a failed acceptance criterion, a defect on a reachable default path, a breach of a declared hard boundary, or data loss. A finding with no real trigger, or one off the default path that breaches no boundary and loses no data, is a note. Findings state their trigger, reach and whether they were observed or inferred, and propose the fix that removes the cause. A test finding names the production change that would pass unnoticed (#3072).
- The principal scoping a stuck repair now says whether the findings share one flaw, compares the structural fix with the small one and recommends one (#3072).

### Chair, inbox and console

- Chairs now delegate substantial work by default, delegated agents may delegate within their own brief, and agents block on `provenant status --wait` or `provenant pr watch` instead of polling (#3062).
- The review ladder now counts reviewers other than the author, in fresh contexts, rather than "independent" reviewers; the required counts are unchanged, and a delegating agent counts as an author of its children's output (#3062).
- The session checkpoint now carries an Intent section and compaction asks the summary for one; the compact hook shows it first and names the host transcript (#3069).
- Global rules now treat a doc, ADR, memory or existing behaviour as evidence of a past decision, and ask agents to name the structural option beside the small one (#3073).
- A spec is now the best current statement of the outcome: implementers raise a flaw with evidence rather than weaken it, and a chair may revise an agent-written criterion through a dated revision that cites the evidence (#3073).
- The risk-tier table no longer has blast-radius or migration rows, and the review-floor ratchet wording is gone (#3081).

### Telemetry and providers

- Cloud jobs can now write. `attempt.cloud` works with `--mode worktree-write` and with delivery targets. The job pushes without force to a named branch (`work_branch`, the delivery branch or the lane branch) and may merge the base branch. The owner settles a writer only after it verifies the push on `origin`, and that branch and head become the lane's result. Hosted Claude may push its `claude/*` branch instead, and the record names the real branch.
- Changed tracked files no longer fail a cloud result, and Codex thread jobs no longer submit with `--read-only`.
- Hosted Claude reports through a `Provenant-Cloud-Result:` trailer commit, so a finished job settles at the next `reconcile`. An unfinished job past its deadline is reported unknown with its session URL. Claude submissions pass `--model` and `--effort`.
- A Codex thread turn settles on its final-answer message even while the service still reports the turn in progress.
- Status shows the requested and observed model, the named and pushed branch, the head and the session URL. A cloud result still does not replace required CI on the exact head.
- Old cloud records are not migrated; settle them with `provenant reconcile --abandon`.

## Additions

- Finished lanes clean up after themselves. Once a lane's work has landed, settlement or the next `gc` pass removes its worktree, local branch, remote branch and remote-tracking ref, but only refs Provenant created, each deletion leased on the tip it checked. Unlanded refs, refs whose landing state is unknown and refs whose tips differ from the ledger are retained regardless of age. Every artefact reports one JSON outcome, and a failed removal warns without failing settlement (#3039).

- Completion cleanup drains final retentions from its queue. Transient failures retry with durable backoff for at most five attempts; exhausted requests stay as diagnostic evidence.

- A graph waiting for a writer lock can resume from its checkpoint before its first attempt has launched, while malformed or unbounded launch waits remain refused. An exhausted writer wait reports its refusal even while other graph nodes are still running.
- Managed installs retain the active release and two recent releases, and manual `gc` collects old terminal graph checkpoints (#3039).
- `lanes.local_cargo` (`all`, `check` or `none`) limits which cargo commands a lane may run locally. The lane's `cargo` shim enforces it for every provider: a refused command exits 1 with one line saying CI runs it and which setting changes this. Set it under `[knobs]`, per run with `--knob lanes.local_cargo=check`, or per graph node with `attempt.local_cargo`. The default is `all`.
- Lanes can check a pushed commit remotely with `scripts/lane-check` before handoff.
- A bundled `light` preset (`attempt.preset = "light"`) makes a lane lighter for read-only or scratch work: shared isolation for unpinned readers, minimal supervision and no delivery receipt. It is node configuration, so it composes with every template and `provenant run --set`. `attempt.isolation` (`private` or `shared`), `attempt.supervision` (`standard` or `minimal`) and `attempt.receipts` (`delivery` or `none`) set each part alone, and match the new `lanes.isolation`, `lanes.supervision` and `lanes.receipts` knobs. Writers keep one writer per worktree, and secret redaction and object integrity stay on in every preset.
- `runs/` now stays under a byte bound. `retention.payload_cap_bytes` (default 16 GiB) caps bulk payload; over it, `gc` evicts the oldest eligible provider state, history, scratch and capture bytes until 75% of the cap. Payload under 24 hours old, or that holds unique private Git objects, an abandonment, a reservation or a continuation, is never evicted. Records, results, exits, receipts and capture tails stay, and `output` and `status` report the payload as evicted. `gc` and `disk report` show pinned and reclaimed bytes.
- Provenant now records, for each branch it makes or reuses, whether it created it or found it there, including branches it pushes, and delivery receipts record the destination's commit before and after delivery. Cleanup can now tell its own landed branches from yours (#3039).
- Review panels can require a minimum number of lenses from a provider family, and individual lenses can require a family while retaining their route pools. `--explain` reports which legs satisfy the requirement or why launch is refused (#3043).

## Fixes

### CLI and install

- Installed skills direct readers to available commands and repository runbooks instead of links that only work in the Provenant checkout (#3063).
- `land --head` accepts any object name that resolves to one commit in the repository, such as a short ID or a branch, and uses its full ID. An ambiguous abbreviation is refused with Git's reason and the corrected command (#3079).
- `pr watch` waits up to three minutes for the first check to be reported after a push, then fails with `CI_NOT_SCHEDULED` naming the missing checks, instead of failing at once with `no checks reported` (#3079).
- `status --wait` now waits for targets to become terminal by default; use `--until any` to return on the first transition.

### Lanes and settlement

- Light writers without a delivery request now state that their work was not delivered (#2886).
- Lane supervisors use less CPU during process churn and make fewer durable writes while lanes wait or run (#2886).
- Waiting lanes avoid repeated provider pipe reads when only a checkpoint or timer wakes their supervisor (#2886).
- Fabric settlement immediately queues detached GC to free completed lanes, with durable retries on each host tick. Disk reporting and GC previews use bounded filesystem samples, and previews reuse run-size accounting. GC can cap the total lane Cargo cache with `gc.lane_cargo_cache_max` (32 GiB by default), retaining live writers and unknown ownership (#3039).
- Continuations inherit their parent’s network bound, commit-pinned source scripts can run, and open-network read-only lanes no longer force Cargo offline (#3040).
- Writers in separate worktrees start concurrently without default host or project lane caps. Lock contention waits until the lane’s launch deadline; one writer per worktree and build-slot limits still apply (#3045).
- Completion cleanup keeps requests and retry state together, recovers dead workers and preserves fresh requeues after exhaustion. Settlement queues cleanup without waiting for a busy dispatch lock (#3050).
- Writer lanes on Linux now seed their Cargo target, registry and Git store with `FICLONE` reflinks on btrfs or XFS, as macOS already did, so a new worktree no longer recompiles third-party dependencies. Where the filesystem cannot clone, the lane builds from scratch and the host prints a warning.
- The prompt every writer lane receives no longer tells it to start with T0 and choose T1 targets. It now says to run one cheap compile check and leave the tiers to CI, which stops lanes queuing `clippy` and `nextest` builds the repository's CI already runs.
- The build-output survey and terminal temporary-payload cleanup now run at admission and GC instead of on the holder's settlement path (#3039).
- Every lane is told to locate before reading: search with line numbers, then read a bounded range, because output stays in the lane's context and is paid for again on each later turn. `orchestrate` and `session` direct chairs to send broad code reading to read-only lanes.
- Every lane with open network, read-only lanes included, now gets GitHub access by default, so lanes can read issues and comment, and writers `git push` to their own branch. Set `lanes.publish = false` to withhold it. `/bin/ps` stays unavailable in a lane because macOS refuses setuid programs under the sandbox; the configuration runbook now says so.
- The host GitHub login lookup retries a timed-out or killed `gh auth token`, reuses the token across fallback routes, and prints a launch warning naming the cause when it fails. Previously a lookup stalled by heavy host load silently left the lane without a token, so `git push` failed with `could not read Username`.
- `run --use review` now builds its pinned source view with one batched object read instead of one Git process per file, so large repositories and linked worktrees whose HEAD differs from the subject no longer fail with a generic preparation refusal. A refusal now says which step failed (tree listing, object read, temporary directory or index build) and whether it timed out, hit an output limit or exited from Git; `status` and `output` show it, and the text no longer points at route and account configuration. The time, file-count and size limits are `domains.run.source_view_seconds`, `source_view_entries` and `source_view_bytes`.

### Accounts, quota and routing

- `route resolve` shows fresh account quota readiness and reports quota admission refusals with their actual causes (#3042).
- Agy accounts can launch again as soon as a fresh quota reading shows headroom after an earlier limit refusal.

### Pipelines and graphs

- Template parameter refusals redact secret-shaped names in diagnostics and structured errors (#3046).
- Cloud writers remain available for later observation when ancestry verification times out or fails to run (#3046).
- Child writer launches leave time to return a registry-contention refusal within the caller’s deadline (#3046).
- Pinned read-only lanes always use a snapshot of the pinned commit, including with `light` or `shared` isolation. Unpinned read-only lanes under `shared` read the bound worktree or current directory live (#3051).
- Review panels retry a malformed lens verdict once and retain returned findings when coverage falls short. If the correction exceeds a prompt limit, the lens remains unavailable and the panel continues under its coverage rule. Coverage failures remain replayable even when finding export also fails. Normal multi-lens findings use the aggregation budget, avoiding premature verification overflow (#3067).
- A review ending in one verdict followed by the closed handoff block, spaced or pretty-printed, keeps its verdict at any result size; a result too large for the retained window loses its findings, not its verdict (#3079).
- An invalid `--set` parameter now fails with the field named and what is accepted. An unknown key lists the template's parameters and suggests the nearest one, a wrong value names the expected shape, and a failure inside a composite also names the node.
