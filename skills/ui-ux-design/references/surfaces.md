# Surfaces

Identify the surface's job before applying visual doctrine.

- **Task/product surfaces** prioritise task completion, familiar controls,
  continuity, applicable states, and recoverability. Density and restraint are
  contextual, not fixed aesthetics.
- **Workspace/operations surfaces** prioritise scanability, stable position,
  keyboard and batch workflows, status legibility, and safe interruption.
- **Data/analytics surfaces** prioritise honest encoding, comparison, units,
  uncertainty, filters, and accessible non-colour meaning. Decoration must not
  compete with data users need to read or act on.
- **Narrative/marketing surfaces** prioritise a coherent argument, distinctive
  identity, evidence near claims, and a clear next step. Landing work is this
  focused context, not a separate global skill.

Many products mix contexts. Assign each region its job and preserve continuity
between them rather than forcing a brand/product binary. Let viewing distance,
content volume, user familiarity, risk, and frequency decide hierarchy and
density.

For narrative paths, connect promise, explanation, product evidence, trust,
objection handling, and action in an order supported by the audience and
traffic context. Do not invent testimonials, customers, metrics, scarcity, or
guarantees. A proof-shaped placeholder must remain visibly a placeholder.

For a data view, begin with the user's question and comparison, then select the
encoding rather than choosing a chart by appearance. Use honest scales, units,
direct labels and context; stress with empty, sparse, dense, extreme, and real
data. Preserve keyboard interaction for filters and marks, and provide an
equivalent text summary or data table when the visual encoding is not enough.
